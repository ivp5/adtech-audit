Hi Aaron,

I'm reaching out because The Markup's investigative ad-tech work
(Citizen Browser, the ANA referencing piece) is the closest model

The audit:

- 76,425 publishers × 4,725 SSPs cited × 28.77M total ads.txt rows
  (6.56M of which are DIRECT-typed) cross-referenced against 739
  SSP sellers.json registries.
- 26.8% of corpus DIRECT claims are PHANTOM (no registry record at
  all); a 100-claim re-verification against current live registries
  produced 95.6% confirmation.
- ~99% of NAMED-registry-matched DIRECT claims have publisher domain
  ≠ registry domain ≠ publisher's parent OWNERDOMAIN — the spec's
  §4.1 "directly controlled by the website owner" definition is
  contradicted across the major SSPs (Magnite/Rubicon classifies
  account 17280 as INTERMEDIARY for Seedtag Advertising SL since at
  least 2020-07; the same DIRECT claim appears on 9,143 publishers).
- SpotX/Magnite — 7,942 publishers carry SpotX DIRECT phantom claims;
  spotxchange.com/sellers.json has returned zero bytes for 5+ years.
  Wayback has zero JSON-200 snapshots.
- IAB Tech Lab's own server returns HTTP 200 + HTML at the spec-
  mandated /sellers.json and /ads.txt paths.

What I've built:

- Stdlib Python reproducer; no project deps. A reader can verify any
  publisher in 60 seconds.
- A self-auditing claims framework that re-derives every quantitative
  claim in the paper from SQL against the corpus DB, with an append-
  only journal of every evaluation.
- Synthetic-corpus falsifiability test: 100% recall on planted
  ground truth for phantom + mismatch + template-injection.
- Live re-verification on 2026-05-09 against 5 SSPs (Seedtag, Taboola,
  Cadent, Rich Audience, IAB Tech Lab itself); findings persist 4
  months after corpus snapshot.

Caveats I'll surface before you ask:

- Corpus is biased toward Tranco top-1M Western commercial sites.
- The "template injection" interpretation of multi-SSP cohort overlap
  is parsimonious-given-evidence, not proven. A self-audit (cycle 412)
  refuted my own paper's earlier strong claim — the "all 7 SSPs are
  phantom-majority" framing was strictly false (0 of 826 publishers
  meet that bar; smartadserver in particular registers as legitimate-
  intermediary mismatch, not phantom). The framework caught my own
  drift; the corrected paper is what's online.
