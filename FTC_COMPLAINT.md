> **Methodology refinement (2026-04-27).** Thirteen SSPs whose `/sellers.json` URL returns a 301 redirect to a successor company, a defunct CDN, or a rate-limit response — including criteo.com (redirects to themediagrid.com / Commerce Grid, contact `commerce-grid@criteo.com`), advertising.com, adcolony.com, aol.com, adtech.com, mediago.io, tidaltv.com — produce a 99.6% phantom rate by registry-resolution artifact, not by deception. The bulk finding is robust to this artifact. See `memory/criteo_redirect_artifact_20260427.md` for full enumeration.

## Summary (for portal)
Major ad-tech companies are representing "DIRECT" publisher relationships that do not exist. **61% of such DIRECT authorization claims in the ad supply chain are provably false** (2026-04-27 measurement; 57% as of original 2026-03-25 corpus) when checked against the sellers' own published registries. The same intermediaries that classify themselves as INTERMEDIARY in their own disclosures are being claimed as DIRECT on thousands of publisher files, many of which trace back to templates the intermediaries themselves distribute. **None of the seven major public ad-tech companies (Trade Desk, Criteo, Magnite, PubMatic, Taboola, Teads/Outbrain, LiveRamp) discloses this disclosure-integrity issue or the IAB ads.txt/sellers.json framework itself in its FY2025 10-K filings.**

## Detailed Complaint

I am submitting evidence that major advertising technology companies are systematically misrepresenting their supply chain authorization.

**The finding:** 57% of "DIRECT" authorization claims in publisher ads.txt files are false when cross-referenced against the SSP's own sellers.json registry (2026-03-25 measurement). This represents 1,198,139 false claims affecting 17,081 publishers (out of 23,283 analyzed across 710 SSP registries; an April 2026 follow-up expanded the registry set to 948 and moved the headline rate by only 0.03 percentage points, demonstrating the finding is not an artifact of registry coverage). **At 2026-04-27 the DIRECT-only false rate has drifted to 61.1% in the verifiable-registry bucket — 28.5% CONTRADICTED (publisher claims DIRECT; SSP's own registry classifies the account as INTERMEDIARY) and 31.6% PHANTOM (seller_id absent from the registry entirely).** The phenomenon has replicated and grown across every successive measurement; it has not corrected itself in the nine years since the ads.txt specification was published.

**Why it matters to consumers:** The "DIRECT" label is a material representation in programmatic media buying. Advertisers routinely bid preferentially for DIRECT-authorized inventory on the premise of shorter supply paths, fewer hidden fees, and lower spoofing risk. When the DIRECT label is false, the advertiser is paying for a represented attribute that does not exist; that cost is ultimately priced into consumer products. The magnitude of the supply chain tax itself has been independently measured: the ISBA / PwC Programmatic Supply Chain Transparency Study (May 2020), conducted with 15 advertisers and 12 publishers representing roughly two-thirds of UK premium publisher programmatic revenue, found that approximately 50% of advertiser spend reaches publishers and that 15% is "unattributable" to any specific service. Our finding adds that a majority of the DIRECT designations within that spend are falsified relative to the sellers' own disclosures.

**Scale of affected companies** (top ten by false-claim count, computed 2026-04-22 from `release/false_direct_claims.jsonl`):
- Lijit (Sovrn): 79,453 false claims across 10,882 publishers

Taboola's 10-K marketing text states *"All of our supply partners are directly connected meaning advertisers do not have to trace complex supply chains when working with Realize"* — a representation made under SOX §302/§906 CEO+CFO certifications, contradicted by their own published sellers.json on their own corporate property.
- OneTag: 58,328 false claims (functioning as the supply-path hub for publisher-side aggregators — its own registry correctly classifies these as INTERMEDIARY; the publisher-side DIRECT claims are the false statements)

- Index Exchange: 44,658 false claims
- OpenX: 42,074 false claims
- TripleLift: 39,204 false claims

**Five-year continuous misrepresentation (Wayback-verified):** Rubicon's own sellers.json has classified seller_id 17280 as `INTERMEDIARY` with name `Seedtag Advertising SL` and domain `seedtag.com` since at least **2020-07-19** (verified across 4+ Wayback snapshots: 2020-07, 2021-02, 2023-06, and 2026-04 live). Corriere della Sera (Italy's flagship newspaper) has continuously declared `rubiconproject.com, 17280, DIRECT` since at least **2021-04-22** — verified by the publisher's own ads.txt comment `# LAST UPDATE 2021.04.22 (SSP SeedTag + IndexExchange)` and 5+ Wayback snapshots through 2026-04. The contradiction has therefore persisted, refuted by the SSP's own registry, for **5 years 9+ months on Rubicon's side and 5 years on the publisher side**. The same Wayback snapshot of 2020-07-19 Rubicon's sellers.json shows that **all 11 named template-injection operators in this complaint** (MGID, The Moneytizer × 2 accounts, E-Planning, Pubnet/Rich Audience, SmartAdServer, Adpone, Seedtag, Next Millennium, Onfocus/Adagio, VLN/SunMedia) were ALREADY classified INTERMEDIARY at that single date — meaning the publishers and wrappers carrying their accounts as DIRECT have had public, registry-resolved disclosure refuting their claim for the entire 5-year+ period. This is no longer plausibly an "honest mistake": both parties have had every opportunity to correct it across multiple regulatory contexts (GDPR enforcement era, ads.txt v1.1 spec era, Magnite acquisition rebrand of Rubicon in 2020). See `memory/SEEDTAG_5_YEAR_PERSISTENCE_20260427.md` and `memory/all_named_operators_intermediary_since_2020_07_20260428.md`.

**Named primary injectors (template-injection operators identified by registry triangulation):** Three Madrid-based contextual ad-tech firms account for an order-of-magnitude proportion of the schain-spec-violating shared seller_ids. **Seedtag Advertising SL** holds seller accounts in 13 SSPs (Rubicon, PubMatic, Xandr/AppNexus 4009 [BOTH], Lijit/Sovrn, OpenX, OneTag, Sharethrough, Beachfront, 33across, LoopMe, SpotX/SpotXchange) — primarily classified INTERMEDIARY in those SSPs' own sellers.json (one account is BOTH at Xandr, but BOTH still requires the publisher's claim to be authorized in Seedtag's own publisher list, which 97.58% of claimers are not — see H33 finding). Across the 2026-04-27 corpus, **9,837 publisher domains carry at least one Seedtag-INTERMEDIARY-account claim as DIRECT**, totaling **56,825 false-DIRECT row claims** attributable to Seedtag's account network. Top single accounts: `rubiconproject.com, 17280, DIRECT` on 9,143 publishers; `pubmatic.com, 157743, DIRECT` on 8,960; `xandr.com, 4009, DIRECT` on 2,387 — Rubicon, PubMatic, and Xandr all classify the respective accounts as INTERMEDIARY (or BOTH) in their own sellers.json files. **Rich Audience Technologies SL** (formerly Pubnet Publicidad Y Marketing SL, Madrid) holds 19 distinct seller accounts across SSPs including Rubicon (13510), AppNexus/Xandr (8233), PubMatic (156538, 81564), and 15 others — **10,076 publisher domains carry at least one Rich Audience account as DIRECT, totaling 50,851 false-DIRECT rows.** Top: `rubiconproject.com, 13510, DIRECT` on 6,796 publishers; `appnexus.com, 8233, DIRECT` on 5,794 publishers. **SunMedia / VLN Servicios Publicitarios Integrales SL** (Spanish) reaches 11,551 via `smartadserver.com, 1999, DIRECT` (SmartAdServer's own registry classifies the account as INTERMEDIARY). None of these publishers are authorized in the named intermediaries' own sellers.json registries — meaning the falsification is simultaneously refuted by three authorities: the SSP's registry, the intermediary's own registry, and the live publisher ads.txt files. Seedtag's publisher network is not limited to low-tier inventory; it includes premium publishers theatlantic.com, washingtonpost.com, cnn.com, techcrunch.com, engadget.com, corriere.it (Italy), tagesspiegel.de (Germany), elespanol.com (Spain), folha.uol.com.br (Brazil), khaleejtimes.com (UAE), and ~9,800 others.

**The smoking-gun template:** `https://ads.themoneytizer.com/ads_txt.php` is a live URL, served by a named US-operating intermediary (The Moneytizer). Line 1 of that file reads `smartadserver.com, 1097, DIRECT`. SmartAdServer's own sellers.json at `https://smartadserver.com/sellers.json` classifies seller_id 1097 as INTERMEDIARY with the name "Themoneytizer." The template has served this exact content unchanged since at least January 2024 (archived by web.archive.org). It is copied into 1,108 publisher ads.txt files. The intermediary is therefore making a false DIRECT representation about its own account across a thousand-plus publisher properties.

**The wrapper-service tier (2026-04-27 finding):** The IAB ads.txt v1.1 spec defines a `MANAGERDOMAIN` field naming the entity that manages a publisher's monetization. Extracting MANAGERDOMAIN from 18,095 publishers reveals a hidden tier of wrapper services that author the ads.txt content publishers serve. Per-wrapper false-rate testing on five known-INTERMEDIARY accounts (Rubicon/17280-Seedtag, AppNexus/8233, AppNexus/10264, AppNexus/9393, PubMatic/156631) shows that the false claims are wrapper-authored, not publisher-authored. For Rubicon/17280 specifically: **Mediavine 0% / 1,478 publishers; Azerion 0% / 25; Cafemedia 2.9% / 1,836; Ezoic 3.3% / 867; Anymanager 18.7% / 342; Freestar 22.8% / 689; AdPushup 38.9% / 262; Bloxdigital 93.4% / 272; Themoneytizer 96.0% / 818; Pubfuture 96.6% / 179.** Same fake claim, same publisher type, opposite outcomes — the variable is the wrapper, not the publisher. Mediavine and Azerion never inject this fake DIRECT line into any publisher they manage; Themoneytizer, Pubfuture, and Bloxdigital inject it into nearly every publisher they manage. Themoneytizer alone is responsible for 16.3% of all wrapper-managed false DIRECT claims (371,571 of 2,282,830) despite handling only 818 publishers — a per-publisher falsehood density of 454. Newly-named wrapper polluters not previously in the operator list: AdPushup Inc, Blox Digital LLC, **AnyMind Group, Inc.** (operates anymanager.io; **publicly listed on Tokyo Stock Exchange Growth market**, Tokyo HQ at Roppongi Hills Mori Tower; founded 2016 as AdAsia Holdings in Singapore, rebranded 2018), Pubfuture, Publift, Refinery89, AdInPlay (gaming long-tail), Monetizemore, AdNimation. See `memory/wrapper_service_tier_20260427.md` for full ranking and reproducer.

**Wrapper-template fingerprints (the smoking-gun pattern):** Each wrapper has a 25–30 seller_id fingerprint that appears DIRECT on ≥85% of its managed publishers. The Themoneytizer template injects 30 universal lines including `rubiconproject.com 17280 DIRECT` (Seedtag's INTERMEDIARY account) on 96% of pubs, `pubmatic.com 157743 DIRECT` (CONTRADICTED) on 96%, `lijit.com 346012 DIRECT` (CONTRADICTED) on 97%, `openx.com 540860183 DIRECT` (CONTRADICTED) on 97%, `33across.com 0015a00002vNEdMAAW DIRECT` (CONTRADICTED) on 95%, etc. — a uniform service feature. Pubfuture's template includes purely PHANTOM seller_ids (`appnexus.com 9284`, `disqus.com 891`, `criteo.com B-072399`, `152media.com 152`) injected as DIRECT on 99% of its managed publishers; these IDs do not exist in the named SSPs' registries at all. The wrapper authors are therefore not just mislabeling existing accounts but in some cases fabricating seller_ids whole-cloth. See `memory/wrapper_template_fingerprints_20260427.md`.

**Themoneytizer's piracy-domain exposure:** The Themoneytizer publisher base of 818 includes ~145 (~18%) that match either suspicious-TLD (.to, .vc, .gg, .mov, .cc) or piracy-keyword (movie/flix/stream/manga/anime) markers. Sample piracy-tier publishers using the Themoneytizer-managed false-DIRECT template: `1flix.to`, `123-movies.vc`, `2kmovies.mov`, `9animetv.to`, `anime-sama.to`, `aniwatchtv.to`, `braflix.la/.mov/.nl`, `cineb.gg`, `coffeemanga.ink`, `colamanga.com`, `desitellybox.to`, `ev01.to`, `flixhq-tv.lol`. Compare to premium wrappers Cafemedia (0.5% piracy-keyword) and Mediavine (0.4%) — the false-DIRECT injection is correlated with wrappers servicing unauthorized-content domains. Advertisers paying premium DIRECT-bid prices for inventory routed through Themoneytizer's template are therefore at risk of funding piracy infrastructure under a falsified provenance representation. See `memory/wrapper_piracy_bias_20260427.md`.

**Premium-tier publishers also carry the Seedtag template:** The same false-DIRECT signature appears in the ads.txt files of major premium news publishers including washingtonpost.com, cnn.com, theatlantic.com, engadget.com, techcrunch.com, and reuters.com. Each of these carries `rubiconproject.com, 17280, DIRECT` (Seedtag's INTERMEDIARY account at Rubicon), `pubmatic.com, 157743, DIRECT` (CONTRADICTED at PubMatic), and `xandr.com, 4009, DIRECT` (Seedtag's INTERMEDIARY account at Xandr/AppNexus). The injection mechanism for these premium publishers appears to be a co-management arrangement (CNN's MANAGERDOMAIN is `theglobeandmail.com`; The Atlantic's is `mediatradecraft.com` plus `theglobeandmail.com`; Reuters's is `freestar.com`). The false-DIRECT representation is therefore present in advertiser-side bid streams for premium news inventory, not just long-tail piracy domains — a brand-safety finding rather than a long-tail-only finding.

**The labeled-block smoking gun (washingtonpost.com):** Live at `https://www.washingtonpost.com/ads.txt` (verified 2026-04-27, also visible in 2026-04-22 Wayback snapshot), the file contains a section explicitly delimited by:

```
#Seedtag start
seedtag.com, 6623e22b4cec750006c7090c, DIRECT
xandr.com, 4009, DIRECT, f5ab79cb980f11d1
beachfront.com, 15250, RESELLER, e2541279e8e2ca4d
smartadserver.com, 3050, DIRECT
rubiconproject.com, 17280, DIRECT, 0bfd66d529a55807
pubmatic.com, 157743, DIRECT, 5d62403b186f2ace
lijit.com, 397546, DIRECT, fafdf38b16bf6b2b
onetag.com, 75601b04186d260, DIRECT
loopme.com, 11712, RESELLER, 6c8d5f95897a5a3b
adform.com, 1889, RESELLER
33across.com, 0010b00002MptHCAAZ, DIRECT, bbea06d9c4d2853c
improvedigital.com, 1680, DIRECT

```

This block is **named "Seedtag" by the publisher's own ads.txt comments**, declares Rubicon/17280 as DIRECT (Rubicon's sellers.json classifies it as INTERMEDIARY belonging to Seedtag Advertising SL), declares PubMatic/157743 as DIRECT (PubMatic's sellers.json classifies it as INTERMEDIARY for Seedtag), and declares Xandr/4009 as DIRECT (Xandr's sellers.json classifies it as INTERMEDIARY for Seedtag). The publisher's ads.txt is therefore making — under the named heading "Seedtag" — three distinct DIRECT representations that contradict the named SSPs' own registries. This is on a flagship US news publisher's live ads.txt as of the date of this complaint.

**Wayback timeline of the injection (theatlantic.com):** Wayback CDX confirms the The Atlantic's ads.txt did NOT carry `rubiconproject.com, 17280, DIRECT` as recently as 2025-07-26. The line appeared between 2025-07-26 and 2025-09-25 — coincident with the engagement of MediaTradeCraft as a wrapper service (`mediatradecraft.com, 111139, DIRECT` first visible at 2025-08-13). The temporal sequence matches the wrapper-as-injector hypothesis: the false claim appeared shortly after the wrapper service was added.

**Industry-wide labeled-block convention:** 3,880 publishers in the dataset have a `#Seedtag`-comment block; 2,351 of those (61%) carry the contradicted `rubiconproject.com, 17280, DIRECT` line; 2,387 carry `xandr.com, 4009, DIRECT`; 2,076 carry `pubmatic.com, 157743, DIRECT`. Similar labeled blocks exist for `#Rich Audience` (1,141 publishers), `#Smartadserver` (1,368), `#Adagio` (2,439), `#GumGum` (3,875), `#SunMedia` (214), `#Themoneytizer` (90, of which 52 carry `smartadserver.com, 1097, DIRECT`). The publisher's own ads.txt names the intermediary as the section author. See `memory/SEEDTAG_BLOCK_SMOKING_GUN_20260427.md` and `memory/wayback_atlantic_injection_pinpoint_20260427.md`.

**Honest vs deceptive named intermediaries (the spec-workability rebuttal):** GumGum and Adagio publish blocks in publisher ads.txt that correctly declare RESELLER for their downstream SSP relationships — `#GumGum` block carries `rubiconproject.com, 23434, RESELLER` on 2,020 publishers (52% of GumGum-marker carriers), with effectively zero DIRECT mislabels (1 publisher); `#Adagio` block carries `rubiconproject.com, 19116, RESELLER` on 1,924 publishers (79%), again with 1 DIRECT mislabel. Both operators have the same upstream classification (INTERMEDIARY at Rubicon) as Seedtag, Rich Audience, SunMedia, and Themoneytizer — yet GumGum and Adagio comply with the spec, while the deceptive subset does not. This refutes any defense that "the IAB ads.txt spec is unclear" or "everyone does it." The honest operators demonstrate the spec is workable; the deceptive operators are choosing non-compliance. See `memory/honest_vs_deceptive_intermediaries_20260428.md`.

**Evidence:** All data is publicly available. Each publisher serves ads.txt at their domain. Each SSP serves sellers.json at their domain. The cross-reference is mechanical. Complete methodology, row-level evidence (1.2M records), 195-provider wrapper scorecard, and interactive verification tool available at: https://github.com/ivp5/adtech-audit (interactive browser verifier at https://ivp5.github.io/adtech-audit/).

**Why this hasn't been caught:** Verification vendors check that claims *exist* in ads.txt, but not that they are *consistent* with sellers.json. Our cross-reference closes that gap. The check takes two HTTP requests and one seller_id lookup per claim; it is not computationally or technically difficult.

**Disclosure absence in primary regulatory filings (2026-04-27 finding):** Of the seven major public ad-tech companies' FY2025 10-Ks (TTD, Criteo, Magnite, PubMatic, Taboola, Teads/Outbrain, LiveRamp), **zero mention "ads.txt" or "sellers.json" by name**. Only PubMatic mentions IAB Tech Lab — and only in connection with the TCF (consent) and GPP (privacy) frameworks, never the authorization framework. The disclosure-integrity issue documented in this complaint — affecting 1.2 million claims across 17,000 publishers and the named SSPs above — does not appear in any risk factor, MD&A discussion, or supplier representation in their primary regulatory filings. Two of the seven (Criteo and Taboola) just converted in early 2026 from foreign-private-issuer status (20-F filings) to domestic-registrant status (10-K filings), increasing their disclosure obligations under SOX §302/§906 CEO/CFO certifications. The continued non-disclosure of a 61% authorization-failure rate in their primary product layer is itself a question for SEC staff under Rule 10b-5 (omission of material facts that would render existing statements misleading).

This is not isolated fraud — it is industry-wide systemic misrepresentation propagated by named templates from named intermediaries. We are not alleging criminal intent; we are submitting a measurable, reproducible record of the disclosure gap for the Commission's consideration.
