ads.txt Framework Integrity Audit

Eight-cycle structural decomposition of the IAB authorization framework.
74,460 publishers × 1,672 SSP registries × 28.77M triples.
Last updated: May 22, 2026.

This page supersedes the March 2026 framing. The original headline ("57.3% of DIRECT claims are provably false") was numerically defensible but the implied story (publisher fraud / industry cartel) does not survive structural decomposition. The deeper finding: 97.5% of phantom claims are attributable to framework brittleness — dead SSPs not propagating, schema migrations not coordinating, orphan registries, confidentiality flags. Only ~2.5% has the "accidental misclaim" shape consistent with individual publisher misconduct. The framework needs a deprecation mechanism, not a fraud investigation.

Headline

Two-vantage rate decomposition (cycles 458-465)

33.83% of publisher DIRECT claims don't reconcile against the named SSP's sellers.json registry. Independently, 4.18% of observed Prebid bidder calls go to SSPs not declared in the publisher's ads.txt (IAB-spec strict per section 5.2.2). Pearson correlation between the two per-publisher: r = 0.15. The two failure modes are nearly independent.

When an SSP gets acquired, rebrands, or fails, the IAB framework provides no propagation channel. ads.txt files maintained at publisher-network level (by Vox/SB Nation, Gannett, Newsquest, Black Press, FanSided, IAC, Townsquare, Booking Holdings, etc.) don't get re-published. Wrapper vendors (Prebid, headerbidding.io) don't push template updates. IAB Tech Lab maintains no SSP-death registry. The phantom rate is geological decay of the framework itself.

Method

Downloaded ads.txt from 74,460 publishers (Tranco top-1M + 186,000-domain crawler harvest). Fetched sellers.json from 1,672 SSPs (~1.89M total seller entries, including Google's confidential-flagged 994K). For each DIRECT claim, looked up the seller_id in the SSP's registry: registry says INTERMEDIARY → contradicted; seller_id absent → phantom; registry says PUBLISHER → plausible. Deduplicated by (publisher, SSP, seller_id) triple. Malformed seller_ids filtered.

Cycle 458-468 added: (1) per-Tranco-tier stratification with Wilson 95% CIs, (2) impression-flow weighting via X-Ray observed activity, (3) X-Ray Prebid bidder-list cross-check for IAB-strict observed-side rate, (4) Pearson correlation of declarative-vs-observed per publisher, (5) SHA1 fingerprinting of phantom seller_id sets for cluster identification, (6) dead-SSP persistence accounting with documented acquisition timelines.

All decomposition queries are reproducible via the public false_direct_claims.jsonl.gz + verify.py. The cross-vantage observed-side calc uses additional capture from a Playwright/X-Ray pipeline (108 publishers in current sample); CI confirms the Pixalate methodology comparison.

Known weaknesses

1. Observed-side sample is small. n=68 publishers with prebid_json captured. Wilson 95% CI on 4.18% = [2.78%, 6.21%]. Pixalate's 13% sits outside this CI, confirming the methodology distinction (declarative vs observed; DSP inclusion).

2. Schema-migration bucket may be undercounted. Beyond Criteo's documented Iponweb→Commerce Grid migration, other SSPs (Yahoo/AOL legacy, Xandr/AppNexus split) likely had quieter format changes that show up here as "live-SSP staleness" rather than the schema-migration bucket.

3. Orphan-registry detection is rate-based. A live SSP with 0 entries in sellers.json gets classified as "orphan registry" at >90% rate. False positives are possible for SSPs with very small genuine publisher rosters; manual inspection of the >90% bucket confirms most are dead-equivalent.

4. Confidentiality flag is IAB-spec-legal. Google's is_confidential: true usage is permitted by IAB sellers.json v1.0 spec. The 178,871 Google phantom claims aren't fraud — they're a known-by-design opacity allowance. We report them in their own bucket rather than rolling into "phantom = fraud."

5. ads.txt freshness. Snapshot dated May 22, 2026 corpus. SSPs can reclassify sellers; phantom claims could resolve if SSPs add entries. Both verdicts are point-in-time.

6. The 2.5% "potential fraud" residual is upper-bound. Many of these are also likely structural (small SSP with incomplete sellers.json, publisher mid-onboarding, etc.) — we keep them in the residual because we haven't structurally identified a mechanism, not because we've confirmed misconduct.
